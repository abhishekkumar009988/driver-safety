Now build the reference images for this film.

⚠️ THERE IS NO "DIVINE GLOW" STYLE AND YOU MUST NOT INVENT ONE.
A glow style is how a film turns into calendar art. If a divine
register is needed, it is carried by MATERIALS, not by light — see
the rule below.

━━━ THEN: THE IMAGE PROMPTS ━━━
Once I pick, write me the actual copy-paste image prompts. I will
paste each one into an image tool myself. Number them and give me a
filename for each so the files stay organised.

⚠️ IF THE REGISTER IS DIVINE OR ROYAL, read this first. It is the
single easiest way to wreck a film, and almost everyone wrecks it.

THE RULE: DIVINITY COMES FROM MATERIALS, NEVER FROM LIGHT.

A god looks divine because of what he is WEARING and what he is MADE
of — heavy raw silk with a deep sheen, bright worked gold with sharp
deep engraving that catches the light, big uncut gems, dense fresh
flowers, skin lit like skin. He does not look divine because the air
is full of golden mist. He looks divine because he is wearing more,
and better, than anyone else in the frame.

The moment you add glow, the image stops being a photograph. These
words are what produce that plastic, computer-rendered, calendar-art
result, and they are BANNED in every prompt you write:

  glowing skin · luminous · radiant · aura · halo · god rays ·
  light shafts · sunbeams through trees · golden mist · golden haze ·
  divine light · ethereal · dreamlike · otherworldly · magical ·
  shimmering · sparkling · floating petals · floating particles ·
  light bloom · lens flare · backlit glow · rim glow · heavenly

Also banned, because they make a god look poor rather than real:
  simple · plain · ordinary · rustic · humble · unadorned ·
  weathered · faded · coarse cotton · no jewellery

So: how a divine character is built instead.

  ❌ CALENDAR ART: "Krishna with glowing luminous blue skin, divine
     aura, god rays streaming through the trees, golden mist,
     floating petals, ethereal dreamlike atmosphere"

  ✅ A PHOTOGRAPH OF A DIVINE FIGURE: "a man of about twenty-two,
     his skin an even deep blue-grey, smooth and unlined with fine
     natural texture and a light sheen along the cheekbone, lit from
     the front left by low sun so the right side of his face falls
     into real shadow; a heavy saffron raw-silk dhoti with a
     hand-woven gold zari border, the silk falling in sharp folds; a
     gold crown of worked metal, bright and polished, the engraving
     cut deep and catching the light, and one peacock feather with
     separated barbs; a thick garland of fresh marigolds and tulsi"

  Read them again. The second one has MORE gold, MORE silk, MORE
  ornament. It is more divine, not less. It just refuses to glow.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
⭐ MUTED IS NOT THE SAME AS FADED — AND THIS TRIPS EVERYONE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Expensive modern footage is NOT colourful. Look at any big film of
the last ten years and most of the frame sits near grey, with one
colour allowed to be strong — skin, or fire, or one piece of cloth.
That is not the same thing as looking old.

  MODERN AND EXPENSIVE          OLD AND CHEAP
  colour pulled toward grey     colour washed out everywhere
  blacks are deep and solid     blacks are grey and lifted
  highlights clean and bright   highlights dull and flat
  sharp, no grain               soft, grainy, hazy
  one strong accent colour      no colour anywhere
  → looks like this year        → looks like a scanned photo

So write: a restrained palette near grey, ONE strong accent colour,
very deep blacks, clean bright highlights, strong contrast, sharp.
Never write: faded, washed, muted-and-soft, grainy, hazy, dusty,
low contrast.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
⭐ THE AGE LINE — WHY EVERYONE COMES OUT LOOKING OLD
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
If you do not state an age, the model picks one, and it almost
always picks older than you meant. Worse, every word used to make
an image look real — pores, lines, weathered, tarnish, worn,
faded, grain, muted — is also a word that means OLD. Use them on a
young character and a twenty-year-old comes back at forty-five,
and the whole film looks like it was shot decades ago.

So every character DNA line MUST open with an age, in years, and
with the words that go with that age:

  ✅ "a woman of about nineteen, smooth unlined skin with fine
     natural texture and a light sheen on the cheekbone, full
     cheeks, bright clear eyes, thick glossy black hair"
  ✅ "a man of about twenty-two, a lean jaw with faint stubble
     only along the edge, smooth forehead, bright clear eyes"
  ✅ "a woman of about sixty, deep lines across the forehead and
     at the eyes, thinning grey-streaked hair, loose skin at the
     throat"   ← ageing words ONLY when the character IS old

For anyone under thirty, these words are banned from the DNA line
and from every image prompt: pores, wrinkles, lines, crow's feet,
weathered, leathery, sun-damaged, rough, coarse, gaunt, sagging,
grey hair, sunken, tired, aged, elderly, mature.

AND THE SAME FOR THE WORLD. If the film is set today, say so in the
world DNA and say it in the picture: clean modern clothing with
sharp new stitching, undamaged paint, working electric light,
present-day haircuts, glass, steel, fresh fabric dye. If the film
is set in the past, say WHICH period and say it through objects —
never through decay. A period is a set of things, not a layer of
dust. Faded, peeling, cracked and crumbling do not say "old era",
they say "low budget".

⚠️⚠️ AND THE BIGGEST ONE: NEVER WRITE THE DEITY'S NAME IN AN IMAGE
OR VIDEO PROMPT.

Write "Krishna" and the model does not picture a person. It reaches
for everything ever labelled Krishna in its training — and that is
overwhelmingly devotional poster art, temple calendars, glowing
murals and 3D devotional renders. The name alone summons the style,
and no amount of camera wording afterwards will beat it. The same is
true of Radha, Shiva, Ram, Hanuman, Durga, and of "Lord", "Shri",
"deity", "divine figure", "God".

So the name lives in the STORY, the plan and the shot titles — where
it belongs and where it helps me follow along. It NEVER appears
inside a prompt I paste into a tool.

Inside a prompt, the character is only ever a physical human being:

  ❌ "Lord Krishna standing under a kadamba tree, divine, devotional"
  ✅ "a young man of about twenty, lean and broad-shouldered, skin an
     even deep blue-grey, shoulder-length curling black hair, a tall
     worked-gold crown holding one peacock feather, a saffron
     raw-silk dhoti with a woven gold border, a bamboo flute in both
     hands, standing under a broad kadamba tree"

Everything that makes him recognisable is still there — the blue
skin, the crown, the peacock feather, the flute, the dhoti. Any
viewer knows exactly who he is. The model, meanwhile, has been handed
a photograph brief instead of a poster brief.

This is what the CHARACTER DNA LINE is for. Write the DNA line so it
never contains the name, then paste it as-is. Put the name only in
the card heading, like "SHOT 07 — Krishna under the kadamba".

⚠️ ALSO BANNED, for the same reason — these are poster words, not
photography words, and every one of them pulls toward a render:
  8K · 4K · ultra-realistic · hyper-realistic · ultra-detailed ·
  highly detailed · masterpiece · premium · devotional · spiritual ·
  majestic · epic · breathtaking · stunning · beautiful ·
  cinematic (used on its own as an adjective) · high budget ·
  IMAX quality · poster · artwork · rendering
Say the lens, the film stock and the light instead. Those are what a
real photograph is described by.

Every divine woman still needs at least: kohl-lined eyes, bindi,
maang tikka, nath, jhumka, choker plus a long necklace, bajuband,
bangles, mehndi, payal, zari silk, a gold-bordered dupatta, fresh
flowers in the hair.
Every divine man still needs at least: a jewelled mukut, tilak,
kundal, vaijayanti mala, kantha, bajuband, kada, zari dhoti,
kamarbandh, anklets, and his signature object.
Describe each of those as a real physical object with a weight, a
weave, a finish and a small imperfection. Never as something that
shines by itself.

━━━ 2A. CAST LIST ━━━
List EVERY person who appears — even for one shot. Parents, friends,

━━━ 2B. THE UPLOAD PORTRAIT — make this one FIRST ━━━

⚠️ READ THIS BEFORE THE SHEET. It prevents two real failures: faces
that come out looking cartoonish, and having to upload a pile of
images for every shot.

For each character produce TWO different things, and they have
different jobs:

  1. THE UPLOAD PORTRAIT — one clean photographic portrait: a single
     frame filling the whole image, one person, every surface blank
     and unmarked. THIS is the only file that ever gets uploaded into
     an image or video tool. Filename: CHAR-[name].png

  2. THE CHARACTER SHEET — the labelled multi-view grid in 2C below.
     This is a PLANNING document for me to look at and for you to
     write the DNA line from. It is NEVER uploaded into a tool.

⚠️ WHY THIS SPLIT EXISTS — THE CARTOON FACE BUG
A model reads an uploaded reference as a whole frame and copies its
LOOK, not just its content. A character sheet is a designed artifact:
a grid, flat studio lighting, labels, colour swatches, several heads
floating on one canvas. Upload that into a video tool and it faithfully
reproduces exactly those qualities — flat, graphic, illustrated. That
is the "it started looking like a cartoon" failure, and it is caused
by the reference, not the prompt. Nothing you write in words will
outvote it.

So the upload portrait must look like a photograph of a real person
and nothing else. Write its prompt like this:

   Photographed on a modern digital cinema camera, 85mm lens at
   f/2.8, eye level, camera height [n] centimetres. A single portrait
   of one person, vertical 9:16, three-quarter view from the waist up.
   Lit by soft window light from the front left at 45 degrees, so the
   left of the face is bright and the right falls into gentle real
   shadow; weak bounce fill from the right. Daylight temperature.
   [the character's full description]
   Standing relaxed against a plain mid-grey wall, facing camera,
   eyes looking into the lens, a calm natural expression, lips
   closed.
   Exactly one person in this frame, no one else, no duplicate, no
   second figure, nobody in the background.
   [the full positive closing block]
   One single frame filling the whole image, one continuous plain
   wall behind, every surface blank and unmarked.
   ⚠️ Write NO ban list here. A tool with one text box reads "no
   grid, no labels, no glow" as a request for grids, labels and
   glow. State only what IS in the frame.

   SAVE AS: CHAR-[name].png
   ✅ DEKHO: ye ek asli insaan ki photo lagni chahiye. Agar isme
   grid, label ya flat studio look hai, dobara banao — wahi cartoon
   wali galti karega.

⚠️ AND NEVER STACK REFERENCES
One upload is stronger than four. Each extra reference image adds its
own texture and style to the blend, and the more you stack the further
the result drifts from photographic. Never upload the character sheet,
the world sheet, the style image and the shot still all together.
 • IMAGE prompts: upload the character portrait only. One per
   character actually in the shot. Nothing else.
 • VIDEO prompts: upload the shot still only — plus the next shot's
   still if, and only if, you are pinning a last frame.
 • The world sheet and style image are for YOU to look at while
   writing. They are never uploaded.

━━━ 2C. CHARACTER SHEET PROMPTS (planning only, never uploaded) ━━━
One per character per age stage. Write each as a ready-to-paste

   Photographic realism, word for word: "healthy skin with fine
   natural texture and a light sheen on the forehead and cheekbones,
   individually visible hair strands, crisp new fabric with a soft

   [STYLE LINE FROM MY PICK — paste it as WORDS. Never upload the
