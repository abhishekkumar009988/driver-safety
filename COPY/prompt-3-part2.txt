     INTIMATE close lenses, almost no camera movement, window light,
              faces fill the frame
     FOLK / DEVOTIONAL  steady unhurried moves, natural light only,
              real materials, the camera respectful and never showy

   Write: "GENRE: Drama. Clips run 4-6s, moves are slow dolly and
   static, light is one soft source, the camera holds on faces."
   And remember: the EMOTION PHASES from the story (㉘) override the
   genre inside the phase they belong to.

⑪ EFFECTS — THE SMALL ONE IN EVERY SHOT, AND THE ONE BIG ONE
   Effect-heavy platform footage looks rich for two separate
   reasons, and most prompts only ever get the first one.

   ① THE SMALL EFFECT — one in every single shot.
   Physical things in the air catching the light. Every shot names
   ONE element in motion:

     dust lifting from under a heel · smoke drifting across a beam ·
     water droplets thrown off a turn · cloth snapping in wind ·
     dry leaves skidding over stone · sand scuffing outward ·
     river spray · embers from a fire · rain on a shoulder ·
     a flock lifting off in the background · steam off hot metal

   Each is a real object with weight that light falls on. They add
   depth and motion and they cost nothing in realism.
   ⭐ And every one of these carries a NUMBER from ⑲. "Smoke drifts"
   is not enough; "smoke level 2, two wisps at chest height drifting
   left to right at walking pace" is.

   ② ⭐ THE BIG EFFECT — EXACTLY ONE SHOT IN THE FILM.
   This is the shot people remember and the shot they send to
   someone else. A film with no spectacle shot is a film nobody
   forwards, however beautiful the rest of it is. Plan ONE, on the
   story's peak, on the same beat as the one bold camera move.

   Choose one, and take it from the story — never decoration:

     FIRE        a wall of flame going up, sparks carried sideways,
                 heat shimmer bending the air behind it
     LIGHTNING   a strike that throws hard white light across her
                 face for a fraction of a second, then darkness
     WATER       a river surging, a wave breaking over stone, water
                 thrown upward in a sheet, rain starting all at once
     WIND        a storm front hitting, cloth and hair pulled flat
                 in one direction, leaves and grit driven past
     EARTH       stone cracking, ground splitting, a shockwave of
                 dust rolling outward along the floor
     SCALE       the camera pulls back and she is tiny against
                 something enormous that was there all along
     TIME        everything in the air freezes mid-fall and the
                 camera moves through it, then it drops
     SKY         clouds moving at impossible speed above a still
                 figure, the light on the ground racing with them
     RISE        ash, petals or embers lifting UPWARD off the ground
                 all at once, against gravity

   ⭐ HOW TO WRITE IT SO IT DOES NOT COME OUT AS A CARTOON. Never
   describe the effect as an effect. Describe it as something that
   happened in front of a real camera, and spend most of the words
   on its CONSEQUENCES on the person:

     ❌ "a magical divine energy blast with glowing particles"
     ✅ "the water goes up in a solid sheet two metres high and
        falls back; the spray hits the left side of her face and
        her eye on that side closes against it; her wet hair is
        pulled flat to her cheek; the light off the water throws a
        moving bright patch across her jaw; the ground around her
        darkens as it lands"

   Four things make an effect photographic, and all four must be
   in the prompt:
     • WHAT IT DOES TO THE LIGHT on her face, and from which side
     • WHAT IT MOVES on her — hair, cloth, eyelids, her balance
     • WHAT IT LEAVES BEHIND — wet stone, scattered dust, a mark
     • HER FACE RECEIVING IT — she flinches, squints, braces. An
       effect nobody reacts to reads as pasted on.

   And give it room: the effect shot is usually the longest clip in
   the film and almost always needs a reaction shot straight after.

   ⚠️ What is still NOT allowed, ever: glow, aura, halo, god rays,
   light shafts, golden mist, lens flare, bloom, sparkle, floating
   luminous particles, divine energy. Those are the calendar-art
   tells. The difference is simple — an EVENT is allowed, a GLOW is
   not. Fire is an event. Lightning is an event. A shining aura is
   not an event, it is a filter. And if a face is behind the effect,
   read ⑳ before you write it.

⑫ THE MOTION PLAN — this is what stops it looking like a slideshow.
   Never write a shot as a POSE. A pose does not move; two poses in
   a row is a swap, not a film.
     ❌ "She kneels at the water, praying."
     ✅ "Already kneeling, she lowers the last flower onto the water,
        her hand staying out over the surface as the ripples spread."
   Three rules for every shot:
     1. START MID-MOTION. The first frame is already moving.
        "already lowering…", "still rising…". Never "she stands".
     2. END MID-MOTION. Never let a clip settle and stop — that full
        stop is exactly what the eye reads as a cut.
     3. THE HANDOVER. The movement at the end of shot N must be the
        SAME movement continuing at the start of shot N+1, seen from
        a new angle. The eye completes it across the cut and the
        join disappears.
          N   ends : "weight shifting onto her right foot, knee
                     beginning to straighten"
          N+1 starts: "from lower, the same knee still straightening"
   Build a handover table: for each shot, the motion at its first
   frame, the motion at its last frame, and that same motion restated
   as the next shot's first frame. The last-frame motion you write
   here becomes BLOCK 3 (the end state) on that shot's card, and the
   next shot's BLOCK 2 must open on that same movement. They must
   match word for word — that is what makes the two clips read as
   one piece. If the motion does not literally
   reappear, that join will look like a swap — rewrite the pair.

   If a join still feels wrong, NEVER add a filler clip. Fix the two
   shots: extend the action so they overlap · re-angle further ·
   move the cut into the busiest part of the movement · let the
   camera keep travelling in the same direction across the cut ·
   carry the ambient sound across.

⑬ THE BEAT TIMELINE — the cure for "it starts right then goes wrong".
   A video tool fills every second it is given. If your prompt runs
   out of instructions at second 3 of a 6-second clip, the tool
   invents seconds 4, 5 and 6 by itself — a second action, a head
   turn nobody asked for, a hand that wanders, a face that changes.
   The clip did not drift. It ran out of script.

   So for every shot, book every single second before writing the
   card. One line per second, and the number of lines must equal
   the clip length exactly:
       4-second clip = 4 lines
       6-second clip = 6 lines
       8-second clip = 8 lines
   Each line is one small physical fact — a hand, a foot, an eye,
   a breath, a shift of weight. Not a feeling. Not a repeat.

   ⭐ AND IF THE SHOT IS A TRAVELLING SHOT, EVERY LINE MUST ALSO SAY
   HOW FAR SHE HAS GOT. This is the difference between a person
   walking and a person walking on the spot.

   A model given only body facts — "her foot lifts", "her braid
   swings", "her arm swings" — will animate a perfect walk that
   covers no ground at all. The legs move, the hips move, and she
   stays exactly where she started, like a treadmill. Everything you
   wrote happened. You just never said she got anywhere.

   So on a travelling shot, every beat names a LANDMARK she reaches,
   passes or leaves behind:

   ❌ WALKING ON THE SPOT — body facts only:
       0-1s: her rear foot pushes off the ground
       1-2s: her braid swings across her shoulder
       2-3s: her lehenga hem sways with her stride
       3-4s: her arm swings forward again
     Every line is true and the clip will go nowhere.

   ✅ ACTUALLY TRAVELLING — distance in every line:
       0-1s: she starts level with the blue door and moves off,
             the door sliding behind her right shoulder
       1-2s: she passes the stacked clay pots, which leave the
             frame behind her
       2-3s: the tethered cow that was far ahead is now beside her
             and falls back out of frame
       3-4s: the neem tree that was a small shape ahead now fills
             the left of frame as she reaches it
     Same walk. Now she has crossed a real piece of ground.

   THE TEST: read your beats and ask "where is she at 0 seconds, and
   where is she at the end?" If the honest answer is "the same place",
   rewrite every line with landmarks.

   HOW TO WRITE IT, three parts per travelling beat:
     1. WHAT SHE PASSES — something in the world going by
     2. WHERE IT GOES — behind her, out of frame left, past her
        shoulder. Things she passes must LEAVE the frame.
     3. WHAT GROWS AHEAD — the thing she is walking toward getting
        bigger, nearer, more detailed

   Also state it once in the prompt, in plain words:
     "She covers real ground — roughly [n] metres across this clip.
      She does not walk on the spot. The background slides past her
      and does not repeat."

   And add to the negatives of every walking clip (in the separate
   negative box if the tool has one, as positives if it does not):
     "no walking on the spot, no treadmill walk, no foot sliding, no
      repeating background, no looping scenery, the subject must
      change position relative to the background"

   Worked example, a 4-second clip that is NOT travelling — here
   body facts alone are correct, because she stays put on purpose:
       0-1s: her fingers close around the door handle
       1-2s: her wrist turns and the latch gives
       2-3s: the door starts to swing inward, her weight follows it
       3-4s: her front foot crosses the threshold, still moving
   Now the tool has nothing to invent.

   ⚠️ If you cannot fill every second with something real, the clip
   is too long for that action. Shorten it. A 3-second clip that is
   fully booked beats an 8-second clip that is half empty. Long
   clips with thin instructions are where drift lives.

⑭ THE CONSTANTS AND THE VARIABLES — per shot, two short lists.
   The tool changes whatever you left unstated, so state it.

   CONSTANTS — must be identical at the first frame and the last:
     the face and its bone structure · hairstyle and how it is tied ·
     every garment and its exact state (pallu on the head or off the
     shoulder — say which) · every piece of jewellery · what each
     hand is holding · the light direction · the time of day
   VARIABLES — the only things allowed to change in this clip:
     usually just the one action, the expression moving one step,
     and the single camera move.

   Write the constants into the card so the lock line can name them.
   ⚠️ If something must genuinely change during the clip — a dupatta
   slipping, a lamp being set down — it goes in the VARIABLES list
   AND in the beat timeline at the exact second it happens. Anything
   that changes without being written in one of those two places is
   a defect.
   ⭐ And every garment state is also carried shot to shot — see ㉗.

⑮ THE THREAD — five things that make it one film, not clips:
   ONE TRAVELLING OBJECT in 5+ shots, changing as the story changes ·
   ONE REPEATING FRAME, ideally the first and last shot ·
   LIGHT MOVING ONE WAY ONLY, never backwards ·
   ONE AMBIENT SOUND in every shot so audio never resets ·
   ONE COLOUR ACCENT only on things that matter.
   State all five, then honour them in every shot.

⑯ THE JOIN PLAN — do this for EVERY pair of neighbouring shots.
   This is the single biggest reason films feel like stitched
   clips instead of one piece. Make a table, one row per join:

     JOIN 01→02 · 02→03 · 03→04 ... one row for every pair.
     Columns:
       • LAST THING MOVING in shot A (the exact body part or object
         and which direction it is travelling)
       • FIRST THING MOVING in shot B (must be the SAME movement,
         continuing, seen from a new angle — or the direct physical
         result of it)
       • WHAT CARRIES ACROSS — tick at least TWO of:
           the same movement continuing
           the same object, still in the same hand
           the same sound running underneath
           the same light direction
           an eyeline in shot A answered by what we see in shot B
           a shape or colour in the same screen position
       • JOIN TYPE: CHAIN (continuous time) or CUT (new time/place)
       • If CUT: which THRESHOLD shot sits between them

   ⚠️ THE ARRIVAL LAW — no scene may simply begin.
   Every new place must be ENTERED on screen by a person. If shot 07
   is the first shot in a new place, then shot 06 or 07 must contain
   the entering — a door pushed, a gate opened, feet stepping in, a
   curtain moved aside, a body walking into frame from the edge.
   Never open a new place on a static waiting frame. The audience
   must feel they walked in with the character.

   ⚠️ THE EXIT LAW — no scene may simply stop.
   Before leaving a place, we see the character begin to leave:
   standing up, turning away, walking out of frame, reaching for
   the way out. The film must never blink from place to place.

   Worked example of a correct join chain:
     shot 4: she fastens her earring, then her eyes flick to the door
     shot 5: her hand closes on the door handle, pulls  ← her look in
             4 is answered by 5; same room, same light
     shot 6: from outside, the door opens and she steps through  ←
             the same door movement, new angle: THRESHOLD
     shot 7: her feet come down onto the road, camera rising with her
             ← the same step continuing
     shot 8: the road opens ahead of her, she stops, breathes  ←
             arrival completed, now the new scene may start
   Five shots to walk out of a house. That is correct. That is what
   makes it feel like one film.

   Fill the join table completely before writing any card. Any row
   where you cannot tick two "carries across" boxes is a broken
   join — fix it by re-angling, by overlapping the action, or by
   adding the missing threshold shot, then renumber.
   ⭐ And between two SCENES, plan the merge shot from ㉓.

⑰ THE OBJECT HANDOFF — the best join there is, and it is free.
   The joins above are good. This one is the one people replay and
   send to somebody. It works like this:

     A glass slips out of a hand. We cut while it is still falling.
     It is STILL FALLING in the next shot — but that shot is a
     different place, a different year, a different scene. The
     glass carried us there.

   One object in motion is the only thing that matches across the
   cut. Everything else changes. The eye follows the object, does
   not notice the change of world, and the two places become one
   piece of film. Nothing else joins two places this strongly.

   PLAN AT LEAST TWO OF THESE PER FILM, and one of them at the
   hardest join — wherever the story jumps place or time.

   WHAT CAN CARRY A HANDOFF:
     something FALLING    a glass, a cloth, a coin, a sword, ash,
                          water poured, a body going down
     something THROWN     a stone, a lamp, a spear, a bundle
     something PASSING    a horse, a cart, a crowd, a bird, a hand
                          sweeping through the near foreground
     something SWALLOWING dust rolling in, smoke, water filling the
                          frame, a cloak crossing the lens, a door
                          swinging shut into the camera
     something TURNING    a wheel, a grinding stone, a spinning
                          coin, a head turning — cut on the turn

   HOW TO WRITE IT, AND IT MUST BE WRITTEN IN BOTH SHOTS:
     • Shot A's END STATE: name the object, which direction it is
       travelling, how fast, and where it sits in the frame at the
       final frame. "The glass has left his fingers and is falling
       through the lower right of the frame, turning slowly."
     • Shot B's FIRST frame: the SAME object, SAME direction, SAME
       speed, SAME part of the frame — but the world behind it is
       the new place. "A falling glass turns slowly through the
       lower right of the frame; behind it, a stone courtyard at
       night instead of the hall."
     • Four things must match or the trick collapses: the object,
       the direction of travel, the speed, and its position in the
       frame. Nothing else needs to match. Nothing else should.

   ⚠️ DO NOT try to make one clip contain both places. That is the
   mush problem again. Generate TWO clips and cut between them in
   the editor, on the frame where the object is in the same spot in
   both. The cut is free; the effect is not.

   ⚠️ And the object must be in the story. A falling glass only
   works if somebody was drinking. An object inserted purely to
   make a clever cut reads as a trick.

⑱ THE SAFE SHOT LADDER — where generators never fail.
   There are shots a generator gets right every single time, and
   shots it gets wrong every single time. Most of a convincing AI
   film is built out of the first kind. This is not a limitation to
   work around; it is the actual craft.

   ALWAYS RIGHT — build the film out of these:
     • HANDS doing one thing — closing, releasing, tying, pouring,
       gripping a hilt, trembling. A hand carries emotion and has
       no face to get wrong.
     • EYES ONLY — an extreme close-up at the level of the eyes.
     • A DETAIL OF AN OBJECT — engraving, a buckle, a bead of water,
       a blade's edge, a thread pulling loose. (But every detail shot
       must do TWO jobs — see ㉖.)
     • A FULL SILHOUETTE against a bright sky or doorway.
     • FEET on ground — stepping, sliding, stopping.
     • SOMETHING FALLING or BLOWING past the lens.
     • THE BACK OF A HEAD with the world in front of it.

   USUALLY WRONG — use rarely and keep them short:
     • a full body walking at medium distance, seen head to toe
     • two people interacting in the same frame
     • any crowd where faces are visible
     • hands doing something complicated — writing, counting
     • a face talking

   ⭐ THE RULE THAT FOLLOWS: for every one wide shot, give the film
   three detail shots. Then the wide shot only has to survive two
   seconds, and the close work carries the emotion. A film built
   out of hands, eyes, objects and silhouettes reads as expensive
   and controlled. A film built out of people walking around in
   full view reads as AI, no matter how good the prompt is.

   And it solves the travel problem too. The FILM must travel —
   through cuts, new places and changing backgrounds. Every SHOT
   must have motion. But motion does not have to mean a full body
   walking: a hand opening, a horse rearing in silhouette, ash
   lifting, a head turning — all of those are motion, and all of
   them are safe.

⑲ ATMOSPHERE NUMBER SYSTEM ⭐ (kitna smoke? kitna fog? — andaza nahi)
   "Some smoke" and "light fog" mean NOTHING to a video model. It
   guesses, and the guess is always too much smoke, too weak a face,
   and a new face invented behind the smoke. So every effect gets a
   NUMBER, and the number means exactly the same thing in every shot
   of this film. The story already chose these numbers in PROMPT 1 —
   your job here is to write the number on every card.

   ⭐ SMOKE LEVEL — the number, then the look, then the direction

    0 — no smoke at all.
    1 — one wisp thinner than a wrist, one tenth of the frame at
        most. The face is completely clean and sharp. Safe for every
        face shot.
    2 — two or three wisps at chest height, drifting at walking pace.
        The face stays clean; only the outline of the shoulders
        softens.  ✅ THIS IS THE HEAVIEST SMOKE ALLOWED IN ANY SHOT
        THAT CONTAINS A READABLE FACE.
    3 — a low band up to the waist, plus two moving columns. Eyes and
        mouth staying sharp; the chin edge may soften.
    4 — smoke up to the neck, columns crossing the face. Eyes and
        brow only. Never in a dialogue shot, never in a face close-up.
    5 — the whole frame filled, the person is a silhouette. No face
        detail of any kind.

   ⭐ FOG LEVEL — the number is the height of the fog ON THE BODY

    0 — none; ground and distance readable.
    1 — ankle height; the feet disappear, the ground still readable.
    2 — knee height; the legs vanish, the body fully clear.
        ✅ Faces must stay at 0, 1 or 2 in fog.
    3 — waist height; faces in the middle distance start to go soft.
    4 — chest height; the person's own face goes soft. Silhouettes
        and wides only.
    5 — full frame; no ground, no distance, no face. A dreamy or lost
        moment only, never a plot action.

   ⭐ DUST / HAZE  1 motes in a beam · 2 dust off the ground under
   footsteps · 3 a beam thick enough to see the light itself · 4 the
   air greys out and the distance is lost.

   ⭐ RAIN  1 a few slow drops on skin, visible only against dark
   cloth · 2 steady drops, visible against dark backgrounds · 3 heavy;
   water standing on the ground, splash at the feet · 4 downpour;
   faces not usable.

   ⭐ FIRE / FLAME — the count, the height in centimetres, and where
   each one is: "two oil lamps, flame six centimetres, one left of
   frame at chest height, one behind her at knee height."

   ⭐ Every effect needs a SOURCE that is in the frame, or was in the
   frame a moment ago: the fire that makes the smoke, the window that
   makes the beam, the cloud that makes the rain, the breath that
   makes the steam. An effect with no source reads as a computer
   mistake.

   ⭐ And the atmosphere is CONTINUOUS: every shot starts at the
   level the previous shot ended on. A change is allowed, but never
   more than one step between two shots, unless something inside the
   shot causes it — and then write the cause: the door opens, the
   fire is thrown, the wind turns.

⑳ FACE REVEAL LAW ⭐ (chehra kab dikhega, kitna dikhega)
   A face hidden behind smoke is where identity dies: the model
   invents new features every time it has to guess. So:

   1. Any shot where the audience must READ the face: smoke 0-2,
      fog 0-2, rain 0-2, dust 0-2. Write the numbers.
   2. The heavy levels (smoke 4-5, fog 4-5, rain 4) are for
      silhouettes, wides and object shots ONLY.
   3. A REVEAL is written as a MOVEMENT, with a direction and a
      duration: "the smoke column drifts to the left and clears her
      eyes and mouth within two seconds, and stays clear for the rest
      of the shot." Never write "the smoke clears" with no direction
      and no time.
   4. The reveal shot gets ONE light source at forty-five degrees
      from the front, with its distance written: "a warm oil lamp,
      chest height, left of camera, one and a half metres away." A
      face revealed against the light stays a shadow, and the model
      fills that shadow with a different face.
   5. AFTER a reveal, the next two shots stay at smoke 0-1, so the
      face is locked in the audience's memory. Never reveal and
      re-cover at once.

㉑ THE TRACE LAW — already stated at the top; this is how it lands
   on the cards.
   • Every card opens with STORY LINE, quoted from my story.
   • Every item of MERI CHEEZEIN from PROMPT 1 must point to a shot
     number. Print that coverage table at the end, like this:

     | MERI CHEEZEIN item | Shot where it appears |
        the flood         | 09, 10, 11
        her silver payal  | 03, 07 (close), 18
        ...               | —

     Any item with no shot is a missing piece — add it now, or write
     one line saying why it cannot be filmed.
   • And the invented table from the top of this prompt, filled.

㉒ NOTHING ADDED ⭐ (apni marzi se cheezein jodna band)
   What is not in my story, my STORY LOCK or my inventory does not go
   into the frame. Not a prop, not an animal, not a visitor, not
   weather, not a crowd, not a decoration in the corner to "fill the
   frame".

   Birth, death, wedding, farewell and other ritual steps: use only
   the steps I actually described. Never add a ritual step that I did
   not write, and never invent a religious object that I did not
   mention.

   If something is genuinely missing and the shot cannot be built
   without it, do NOT invent it silently. Do this instead:

     1. Build the shot using the most plain, most neutral choice
        possible.
     2. Write that choice at the end, in a block called MY ASSUMPTIONS,
        one line each:
          MY ASSUMPTIONS — 1. she is standing, not sitting (story did
          not say). 2. the doorway is wooden (the location DNA did
          not say).
     3. Keep that block at the very end of your answer, after the
        cards and after the tables.

   I will read it and correct it. A written assumption I can fix in
   one minute. A silent invention costs me a whole day of failed
   generations.

㉓ MERGE SHOTS — the short shot that joins two scenes ⭐
   (Tumhara "beech beech ke seenshots" — ye device hai.)
   Between two scenes, and between two big emotions inside one scene,
   insert ONE short shot, about 4 to 6 seconds, that belongs to both
   sides. It must contain two of these at the same time:

     · the same movement continuing (a turn that becomes another turn)
     · the same shape (a circle, a line, a curve repeating)
     · the same colour passing from one object to another
     · the same action done by two different people
     · the same eyeline handed over
     · the same emotion changing owner

   Say in the card: JOINS Scene 2 → Scene 3. Name which two of the
   six devices it uses. If it does not join two things, it is not a
   merge shot — cut it.

㉔ HOLD, DRAPE AND WEAR MECHANICS ⭐
   (Tumhara "kab kaise kisko hold krna hai kaise odhna hai" — yahi hai.)
   This is where AI clips fall apart. Never write "they embrace" or
   "she adjusts her dupatta". Write the physical steps, in order, with
   the limbs named.

   HOLDING: which hand, where it grips, how hard, and what the other
            hand does at the same time.
            "Her left hand closes on his right forearm just below the
            elbow, thumb on the outside; her right hand stays at her
            side."
   DRAPING: lift → travel → land. Three steps, three timestamps.
            "She lifts the cloth with her right hand to shoulder
            height, carries it across her chest, and lays it over the
            left shoulder; it settles and stays."
   WEARING: what moves when the body moves. Sleeve, hem, veil, hair.
            "When she turns, the hem sweeps a quarter turn behind her
            and settles."
   TOUCHING: contact must be visible for at least one full second,
            and the second person must react to it — a breath, a
            blink, a small change of weight.

   Never let a cloth, a hand or a garland pass through a body, and
   never let it change from one shoulder to the other between shots.

㉕ CAMERA COVERAGE — 4 TO 6 SETUPS, VERIFIED ⭐
   Choose 4 to 6 setups for the whole film and never invent a seventh.

     A — WIDE / master: the whole place, tiny people, one landmark
     B — MEDIUM: waist-up, two people if they are together
     C — CLOSE: face, or hands doing the important thing
     D — OVER-SHOULDER: we look past one person at the other
     E — HIGH or TOP-DOWN: the ground, the spread, the arrangement
     F — LOW: from knee height looking up, for power moments only

   Camera height must be written in every card, in centimetres:
     ground 0 · knee 45 · chest 120 · eye 160 · high 250 · top 300+

   Laws:
     · Every scene must be covered from at least FOUR of the setups.
     · Never two shots in a row from the same setup, unless the second
       is a deliberate hold on the same face.
     · Exactly ONE bold camera move in the entire film (see ⑧).
     · After a bold move, the next shot is locked or nearly locked.
     · Print a small coverage table at the end: setup letter × how
       many shots use it. Any setup used fewer than four times, or a
       scene sitting in one setup, is a defect — fix it.

㉖ A DETAIL SHOT MUST DO TWO JOBS ⭐
   Every close-up of an object must (a) be the RESULT of the previous
   shot and (b) SET UP the next one. Write both jobs on the card:

     DOES:  the knot is already tightening because of Shot 05.
     SETS:  the same rope is what Shot 06 pulls.

   A pretty object with no before and no after is filler. Delete it.

㉗ THE COSTUME STATE LINE ⭐ (kapda actor hai, lock nahi)
   Never write "she wears the same clothes as before". Write what the
   clothing is DOING in this shot, and keep the state continuous:

     · at the start of the shot, the state it entered with (wet hem,
       loose sash, hair forward on the left, pallu on the head)
     · what it does during the shot
     · the exact state it leaves in, so the next card can start there

   Print it on every card as: CLOTH STATE — starts / changes / ends.
   If two neighbouring cards disagree about a garment's state, that
   is a defect — fix the pair.

㉘ THE PHASE ENGINE ⭐ (quality, VFX aur look story ke emotion se
   automatically badle — tumhara main point)
   The film has 3 to 5 EMOTION PHASES, already named in the STORY
   LOCK. They are law. Every card states its phase, and the phase
   chooses the look for you — I do not want to decide it shot by shot:

     peace:  wide, soft light, greens and cream, slow drift, no VFX
     want:   closer, warmer, one loud colour, light from one side,
             small natural VFX already building (dust, drifting petals,
             heat)
     test:   tighter, cooler, harder shadow, faster drift, weather VFX
     break:  closest, darkest, hand-held, the ONE big VFX event lands
             HERE
     grace:  opens wide again, bright clean light, the loudest colour
             of the film, calm locked frame

   Rules:
     · The phase overrides the genre mode: inside "break", the light
       is harder and the moves are shorter even if the genre is Drama.
     · The look must stay consistent INSIDE a phase, so the audience
       feels the change when the phase changes.
     · A phase change lands on a shot, not in the middle of one, unless
       the change is caused by something inside that shot — and then
       write the cause.
     · Print the phase on every card, and print a small phase table
       before the cards: phase · shots · look · effects.

㉙ FRAME INVENTORY ⭐ (ginti likho, warna model bana dega)
   Every card carries a count. What is not counted does not exist in
   the frame, and if the model wants something, it must ask me first.

     PEOPLE   — how many, and where each one stands (left / centre /
                right, front / middle / back), with the distance in
                metres.
     LIGHT    — how many sources, what each one is, where it is, and
                its distance in metres.
     OBJECTS  — every object, with its number.
     CLOTH    — how many pieces on each person, and in what state.
     DISTANCE — how far the camera is from the subject, in metres.
     HEIGHT   — which part of the body sits at the centre of the frame.

   Write it straight onto the card:
     FRAME INVENTORY — 2 people (her, centre, 1.5 m; him, left, 3 m) ·
     2 lights (lamp, left, 1 m; fire, back right, 4 m) · 3 objects
     (one brass thali, one folded cloth, one rope) · smoke level 2

   AND THE RULE THAT MAKES IT WORK: whatever the Image prompt says
   must already be in this inventory. If the image prompt mentions an
   object that is not counted here, the card is wrong — either count
   it, or take it out of the prompt.
