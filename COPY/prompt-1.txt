RULE 2 — MY THINGS FIRST  ⭐ v9
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Before planning anything, list every solid thing I gave you as a

Do not smooth me out.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 3 — STORY FIRST, LOOK SECOND  ⭐ v9 (sabse bada fix)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Never decide location, light, colour, lens or effects first and then
fit the story inside them. That produces pretty frames with no feeling.

Work in this order:
  A) Write the emotional flow: for each beat — what happens, what the
     character feels, what the other character does back.
  B) ONLY THEN derive the look from that feeling:

  FEELING                     →   WORLD / LIGHT / COLOUR / CAMERA
  first meeting, shyness      →   warm close light, faces close, slow
  playful, teasing            →   green outdoors, moving fabric, sun
  happiness, celebration      →   flowers, brighter, more air, wider
  devotion, wonder            →   smoke, flame, deep warm light, slow
  longing, sadness            →   soft close-up, lowered gaze, still
  reunion, joy                →   open space, wide shots, flowing cloth
  anger, refusal              →   hard light, deep shadow, tight frame

So the theme, the palette and the VFX change WITH the story. They are
not fixed at the start.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 4 — THE FILM MUST TRAVEL
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Nobody may teleport. Every move from one place to the next is its own
shot. This is what makes a film feel like ONE film instead of separate
clips.

  ❌ WRONG: she is getting ready at home → she is dancing on the road
  ✅ RIGHT: getting ready → she turns to the door → she pulls the door
     open → she steps over the doorstep → she walks down the lane →
     she reaches the road → she stops → she begins to dance

Six shots to get from the house to the road. That is correct. Those
in-between shots are not filler — they are the glue.

Rules:
- Every character's ENTRY is shown. Nobody appears from nowhere.
- Every character's EXIT is shown. Nobody vanishes.
- Every door, gate, step, path or turn between two places gets its own
  shot, with a person physically doing something in it.
- If someone is sitting in one shot and standing in the next, the
  standing up is a missing shot — add it.
- If someone holds something, track it: where it came from, who has it,
  where it ends up.
- No single place gets more than 3 shots in a row.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 5 — THE PROGRESS LAW  ⭐ v9
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Walking is not progress. Progress means the background behind them
changes. The viewer reads the journey from what passes BEHIND the
person, not from the person's legs.

Every walking or moving shot must have all three:
  1. Something left behind — named, with the second it exits frame
  2. Something ahead getting bigger — a landmark that grows
  3. The subject's size or position changing — they get smaller if
     they walk away, larger if they approach

Test: swap shot 7 with shot 4. If nobody can tell the difference, both
shots are useless. Delete one, or give them different ground.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 6 — THE PERFORMANCE ENGINE  ⭐ v9 (acting ka asli fix)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
For every shot where a face is visible, write these six things. Never
write a bare emotion word — "smiles", "looks sad", "reacts", "divine
expression" produce a blank, fake face.

  THOUGHT      — what they are thinking, in their own words.
                 Not "she is shy". But: "he will notice and I know it."
  EYE          — where the eyes are, and the exact second they move.
                 "eyes down on the floor for 2 seconds, then a fast
                 glance up to his face, then straight back down"
  ARRIVAL      — how the expression ARRIVES. Not the expression itself.
                 "the smile starts at the left corner of the mouth"
  CONTROL      — the attempt to hide it. "she presses her lips flat to
                 stop it"
  LEAK         — one careless thing that gives it away. "her breath
                 escapes in a short laugh she did not plan"
  BUSY BODY    — what the hands are doing while the face is feeling

⭐ Two laws that matter more than all the above:

  1. THE CAMERA IS NOT THE AUDIENCE. The character performs for the
     OTHER CHARACTER, never for the lens. Nobody looks at the camera.
     Nobody holds a face for the camera. If a person is alone, they
     perform for the thing they are looking at — a door, a river, an
     empty road.

  2. SUPPRESS, DON'T DISPLAY. A person who cries on cue looks computer
     generated. A person who tries NOT to cry looks real. Write the
     attempt to hold it in, and one small leak.

Sadness is never frozen. It arrives in steps:
  eyes stop moving → breath goes shallow → the jaw sets → the lower lip
  tightens → the eyes fill but do not spill → blinking slows

Anger is never a shout. It is:
  jaw visibly set → nostrils flaring on the inhale → hard unblinking
  stare → chin lowered → breathing hard through the nose → body rigid

Happiness is never a fixed smile. It is:
  the eyes soften first → then the cheeks lift → then the mouth opens →
  then the head tips → then the hand rises to cover it, half a beat late

⭐ Put the intensity on a scale of 1 to 10, and always write one level
HIGHER than you want. The render always comes back one level lower.
And: the biggest emotion gets the CLOSEST shot. In a wide shot a face
becomes generic — save the wide for the body, the close-up for the
feeling.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 7 — THE RELATIONSHIP ENGINE  ⭐ v9 (do log ek frame me)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Whenever two people share the frame, something must be RUNNING between
them. A still pose between two people is a dead shot.

Every scene with two people needs all four:
  INTENTION    — what each one wants from the other, right now
  ACTION       — the physical thing one of them does about it
  REACTION     — what the other does back, and how LATE it comes
  CONSEQUENCE  — what has physically changed by the end of the scene

⭐ REACTIONS ARRIVE LATE. This one rule is worth more than any prompt
   wording. If he reaches for the cloth, she does not smile at the same
   instant. She notices, keeps still for a beat, THEN the smile comes.
   Give the reaction its own shot, after the action.

The running actions available to you — use one per scene, never two:
  touch before touching · glance away · be caught looking · pretend not
  to notice · adjust their clothing · offer help · shield them · wait
  for them · match their movement · tease · refuse · relent · notice
  something small and keep it to themselves

⭐ After every important action, give the next shot to the FACE that
   received it. Action → the other person's reaction. Always.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 8 — COSTUME IS AN ACTOR, NOT A LOCK  ⭐ v9
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Writing "same dupatta in every shot" is not enough. A garment that
never responds is a sticker.

For every major garment, write its STATE at the start, and what changes
it — and the change must be caused by an action on screen:

  "At the start the green veil covers the back of her head and falls
   over her right shoulder. He lifts only the gold-edged corner with
   two fingers. She notices the fingers, looks down, and lets the
   corner fall. In the next shot the same corner swings across the
   lower right of frame as she turns — the fold, the border and the
   flower in her hair all continuous."

Rules:
- A garment never changes state by itself. Someone or something moves it.
- Jewellery moves with the body, catches light, and can be touched,
  held, or dropped. A dropped piece of jewellery is a story event.
- If the costume changes during the film, the change is SHOWN happening.
- Same character = same clothing state at every cut, unless the change
  was filmed.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 9 — DETAIL SHOTS ARE NOT DECORATION  ⭐ v9
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Every close-up of a hand, a foot, a piece of jewellery, fabric or an
object must do TWO jobs:
  1. It shows the RESULT of the previous shot
  2. It sets up the NEXT shot

A beautiful shot with no action, no reaction and no consequence is a
defect. Delete it.

These are the detail shots that carry a film:
  feet walking · a dropped ornament on the ground · hand taking hand ·
  a veil edge being adjusted · eyes close-up · a garland moving ·
  fingers on an instrument · jewellery swinging · a face receiving
  something · the space between two people

Minimum per scene: one face reaction · one body action · one
relationship response · one physical consequence.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 10 — THE SIX MERGES  ⭐ v9 (do scene jodne ke tarike)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
A cut between two shots disappears when something matches across it.
Use one of these six. Never just cut and hope.

 1. MOVEMENT MATCH — she is walking in shot A; shot B opens with the
    same walking continuing in the same direction and speed.
 2. SHAPE MATCH — the curve of the green veil in one shot becomes the
    curve of the tree line in the next.
 3. COLOUR MATCH — green cloth → green forest · gold jewellery → warm
    sunlight · white flowers → white highlights.
 4. ACTION MATCH — a hand reaches out in one shot; the next shot opens
    with the same hand taking something.
 5. EYELINE MATCH — she looks up; cut to what she sees; cut to his
    reaction. Three shots from one look.
 6. EMOTIONAL MATCH — she smiles; cut to him receiving the smile.

Plus two object laws:
  OBJECT HANDOFF — an object leaves the frame mid-motion in one shot
  and is still moving in the next shot from a new angle. Match the same
  object, same direction, same speed, same position in frame. The place
  may change completely — that is the magic. Do NOT generate both in
  one clip; cut them in the editor at the frame where they align.
  OBJECT CONSEQUENCE — an object shows the RESULT of an action:
  jewellery falling to the ground after a hurried step.

Every film needs at least two handoffs. One of them goes at the biggest
jump in the story.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 11 — CAMERA: 4 TO 6 SETUPS, CHOSEN BY EMOTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Plan four to six camera setups for the film and reuse them. That is
what makes separate clips look like one shoot.

Choose the size from the emotion, never from variety:

  reaction, small feeling      → medium close-up, camera still
  the biggest moment           → extreme close-up, camera still
  power, divinity              → low angle looking up
  shame, defeat, guilt         → high angle looking down
  two people's relationship    → two-shot or over-the-shoulder
  loneliness                   → wide, the figure small in frame
  travel, movement             → full body or wide, often from behind
  detail, object, hand         → insert, camera static
  transition, scale            → overhead or drone

⭐ Always state how high the camera is off the ground. If you do not,
it renders flat at chest height every time and the shot loses meaning.
   eyes 20 cm · ground 40 cm · knee 70 cm · chest 150 cm · eye level
   165 cm · above head 200 cm

Camera moves — name them, and give each one a speed and a distance:
  Static · Dolly In/Out · Truck Left/Right · Pan · Tilt · Crane Up/Down ·
  Arc · Orbit · Follow · Overhead Rise · Crash Zoom · Whip Pan

⭐ Only ONE bold move in the whole film. A bold move is a punch. One
   lands. Six is just noise. Everything else is static, dolly, truck,
   pan or follow.

⭐ One camera move per clip. Never two stacked. And every move needs a
   speed: "Dolly In, slow, 1 metre."

Two more laws:
- SCREEN DIRECTION: the whole film travels one way — left to right. If
  they exit frame right, they enter the next shot from frame left. The
  eye then knows where they are going and the cut disappears.
- THE 30 DEGREE RULE: never cut between two shots of the same subject
  that are less than 30 degrees apart, or it reads as a jump cut.
- Never give two neighbouring shots the same size AND the same move.

And the relationship between camera and subject must be stated whenever
both are moving, or the subject glides and the world stands still:
  "The camera follows behind her at her walking pace, holding the same
   distance, while the lane slides past on both sides."

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 12 — VFX COMES FROM THE FEELING  ⭐ v9
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Never add particles for decoration. Every visual event is tied to what
the character is feeling, and to something happening.

Decide the film's effects like this:
  flowers, petals, drifting blossom   → happiness, romance, play
  sunlight through leaves             → a magical meeting, warmth
  soft smoke, haze                    → devotion, a sacred pause
  flame of a lamp                     → stillness, prayer, a vow
  moving veil, scarf, cloth           → attraction, motion, transition
  a garland moving                     → a relationship being touched
  falling jewellery, a dropped object  → the result of an action
  rain, water splash                   → a refusal, a break, a shock

Small event: ONE in every shot. Big event: ONE or TWO in the whole
film, at the peak. Two big effects eat each other.

⭐ Write the effect as its RESULT, never as the effect itself:
  ❌ "magical divine energy blast with glowing particles"
  ✅ "the water rises in a two-metre sheet and falls; drops land on her
     left cheek and that eye closes; wet hair sticks to her cheek; a
     moving bright wet patch of reflected light crosses her jaw; the
     ground turns dark around her"

Four things must be in every big effect: where the light hits the face ·
what it moves on them · what it leaves behind · how the face takes it.

⭐⭐ Write negatives as POSITIVES. My tool has no separate negative box —
   everything I type is read as a thing to make. "No glow" creates glow.
   So never write what you do not want. Instead:

  ❌ no god rays, no aura, no glow, no bloom, no godlight
  ✅ clear dry air; every surface lit only by one real light source and
     its own cast shadow

  ❌ no plastic skin
  ✅ skin with fine natural texture, an even matte surface, slight
     natural unevenness in the colour

  ❌ no text, no watermark
  ✅ every surface blank and unmarked

  ❌ no extra people
  ✅ exactly one person in the frame (and then say how many, as a number)

⭐⭐ RULE 13 — EVERY EFFECT GETS A NUMBER  ⭐ v9 (kitna smoke? kitna fog?)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
"Some smoke", "light haze", "a bit of fog" mean nothing. The model
guesses, and its guess is always too much smoke and too weak a face. So
every effect carries a NUMBER, and the number means the same thing in
every shot of the whole film.

   smoke 0-5 · fog 0-5 · dust 0-4 · rain 0-4
   fire: how many flames, how tall in centimetres, and where each one is

⭐ AND THIS DECISION IS MADE HERE, IN THE STORY. Do not leave it for a
   later step. Whatever will be seen on screen gets its amount now:

   ✅ "during the bath, heavy steam — smoke level 2, and the face stays
      completely clean"
   ✅ "the curse scene hides her face — smoke level 5, silhouette only,
      and the reveal comes later"
   ✅ "rain level 3 — water stands on the ground, drops visible on a
      dark background"
   ❌ "some smoke", "light rain", "a little haze"

Nothing in front of a face ever goes above level 2 in a shot where the
face must be read. The full table and the reveal law are in PROMPT 3 —
but the choice is made here, in the story, and it goes into the
EMOTIONAL FLOW and into the STORY LOCK as a number.
