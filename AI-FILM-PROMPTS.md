# 1️⃣ PROMPT 1 — STORY

> **Kahan paste karna hai:** ChatGPT / Gemini / Claude (web search ON ho to best)
> **Neeche kya jodna hai:** Sabse aakhir me apni kahani — `MY STORY` wale box me.

```
You are a film director, screenwriter, researcher and prompt engineer.

I am making a short vertical film (9:16, Instagram Reel). I will paste your
prompts into an image tool and a video tool myself. You are not generating
pictures — you are WRITING the prompts I will paste.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 1 — I GIVE THE STORY, YOU GIVE EVERYTHING ELSE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Look at MY STORY at the bottom of this message. If it is empty, or still
shows bracket text, or is too vague to film, then STOP and reply only with:
  "Mujhe aapki kahani chahiye — 2-4 line me likh dijiye, jo yaad hai."
Then wait. The story is the one thing I must give you.

Other than the story, NEVER ask me anything. Not about emotion, tone, mood,
ending, style, pacing, music, audience, brand, camera, look, or reference
images. I have nothing to give you. Derive all of it yourself.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 2 — RESEARCH IT, THEN COUNT MY THINGS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
My story is rough, from memory, and probably has mistakes. Research what
actually happened. Correct my errors. Use real names, the real place, the
real sequence, the real period. Never invent events. If two versions exist,
pick the most widely accepted one and say which.

Then, BEFORE writing anything, make MY THINGS — a numbered list of every
solid thing I put in my story: every person, every place, every object,
every action, every image I mentioned.

RULE: every single thing on that list must appear in the finished film.
You may move it earlier or later, you may add around it — you may NOT
drop it, replace it, or "improve" it away. Do not tell me a thing is
hard to film. Do not say a thing would look better differently.

The most common failure is this: my rough, fast, odd idea gets smoothed
into a generic pretty film that is no longer mine. Do not do that.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 3 — THE FILM MUST TRAVEL, AND EVERY STEP MUST BE SHOWN
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Nobody may teleport. Every move from one place to the next is shown.

  ❌ WRONG: she is getting ready at home → she is dancing on the road
  ✅ RIGHT: getting ready → she turns to the door → she pulls the door
     open → she steps over the doorstep → she walks down the lane →
     she reaches the road → she stops → she begins to dance

Rules:
- Minimum 4 different locations in the film.
- No location gets more than 3 shots in a row.
- Every character's ENTRY is shown. Nobody appears from nowhere.
- Every character's EXIT is shown. Nobody vanishes.
- Every door, gate, step, path or turn between two places is its own shot,
  with a person physically doing something in it.
- If someone is sitting in one shot and standing in the next, the standing
  up is a missing shot — add it.
- If someone holds something, track it: where it came from, who has it,
  where it ends up.

⚠️ THE PROGRESS LAW — this is what stops the film standing still:
Walking is not progress. Progress means the background changes behind her.
  • Something must be LEFT BEHIND in every travelling shot — named, with
    the second it passes out of frame.
  • Something ahead must GET BIGGER — a tree, a gate, a temple, the river.
  • The ground must change — packed earth, then dust, then wet mud, then sand.
  • The distance must be stated — "about 8 metres", "past four doorways".
  • If a shot starts and ends in the same place, that shot is wrong.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 4 — HOW MANY SHOTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Most tools make 8-second clips. For a 45-second film write 18-22 shots.
For 30 seconds write 13-16 shots. Never fewer than 12. Work it out and
tell me the number before you start.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
NOW GIVE ME YOUR OUTPUT IN THESE FIVE PARTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
┃  PART A — WHAT I GAVE YOU AND WHAT YOU CORRECTED        ┃
┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

  MY THINGS: the numbered list, as described in Rule 2.

  WHAT REALLY HAPPENED: your research, and what you corrected in my
  version. Name my mistakes plainly.

  THE FILM IN SIX LINES: what happens, first to last, in plain words.

  THE EMOTIONAL ARC — one line per act, and name the exact turn:
    Opening feeling  →  what changes  →  final feeling

┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
┃  PART B — THE THREE DIRECTIONS                          ┃
┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

  Give me THREE complete, different ways to tell this story. Every one
  must contain every single thing from MY THINGS. They differ only in
  whose eyes we see it through, and where we come in.

  For each direction:
    • Whose eyes: one line
    • Opening image: what is on screen at 0 seconds
    • The turn: the one moment everything changes
    • Closing image: what the last frame is
    • What the audience feels: one line
    • Why it works for this story: one line
    • The risk: one honest line

  Rank them. Put ⭐ on the one you would shoot.

  Then STOP and let me choose. Do not continue past this point until I
  reply with a number (or "go" for your starred pick).

┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
┃  PART C — THE ROUTE MAP                                 ┃
┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

  A table of the journey. One row per leg:

  | # | Place | Shots here | How we ARRIVE (the threshold shot) | How we LEAVE (the exit shot) | What is BEHIND HER here | What is ahead and getting bigger |

  Below it, one line: "This film travels: [place 1] → [place 2] → ..."

  There must be at least 4 places. No place with more than 3 shots in a row.

┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
┃  PART D — HOW THE THEME CHANGES WITH THE STORY          ┃
┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

  The film must not look the same all the way through. As the feeling
  changes, the world changes with it.

  A table:

  | Story phase | What the audience feels | Location changes to | Light becomes | Colour does this | Natural activity in the air |

  "Natural activity" = drifting petals, moving leaves, smoke, dust in a
  beam, water rippling, embers. Real physical things. Not glow, not aura,
  not light rays from nowhere.

  The world gets WARMER and MORE OPEN as the film gets happier. It gets
  tighter and quieter as it gets sadder. This happens by itself — I do
  not want to see the same street in the same light in shot 3 and shot 17.

┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
┃  PART E — STORY LOCK  ⭐ THE MOST IMPORTANT OUTPUT      ┃
┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

  This block goes into every other tool. It is the only thing carrying
  my story forward. Write it plainly, no decoration.

  ────────────────────────────────────
  STORY LOCK
  ────────────────────────────────────
  FILM: [title] · [runtime] · 9:16 vertical

  LOGLINE: [one sentence]

  THE FILM IN SIX LINES:
  1. [ ]
  2. [ ]
  3. [ ]
  4. [ ]
  5. [ ]
  6. [ ]

  WHO WANTS WHAT: [character — what they want — what stops them]

  THE TURN: [the exact moment everything changes]

  THE ENDING IMAGE: [what the last frame shows]

  OPENING FEELING → FINAL FEELING: [ ]

  THE ROUTE: [place 1] → [place 2] → [place 3] → ...

  CAST TIMELINE:
  | Event # | What happens | Character A | Character B | Character C |
  (Use only: ABSENT · ARRIVES · ON · OFF · GONE)

  THE THEME SHIFT:
  | Phase | Feeling | Location | Light | Air |

  NEVER CHANGE THESE — if any of these change, the film is a different film:
  1. [ ]
  2. [ ]
  3. [ ]
  4. [ ]
  5. [ ]
  ────────────────────────────────────

  Then say: "STORY LOCK ready. Paste this into PROMPT 2."

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULES YOU MUST FOLLOW WHILE WRITING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
✗ Never ask me about style, mood, emotion, tone, ending, music or camera.
✗ Never ask me for a reference image or an upload.
✗ Never drop anything from MY THINGS.
✗ Never say a thing is hard to film.
✓ Derive every decision from the story itself.
✓ Write in Hinglish explanation, English prompts.
✓ Nothing may happen that I did not put in the story, unless you tell me
  clearly in PART A what you added and why.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
MY STORY (rough, from memory — research it and fix it)
⚠️ If this line is empty, stop and ask me for it.
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

[YAHAN APNI KAHANI 2-4 LINE ME LIKHO]

RUNTIME: 45 seconds
```

# 2️⃣ PROMPT 2 — CHARACTERS + DUNIYA

> **Kahan paste karna hai:** Usi chat me jahan PROMPT 1 chalaya
> **Kya jodna hai:** STORY LOCK (PROMPT 1 ka PART E)

```
You are a character designer and production designer for a live-action film.

I will paste the STORY LOCK from our earlier conversation. Read it fully.
You are writing the reference prompts I will paste into an image tool myself.
You are not generating pictures.

⚠️ NEVER ask me for a reference image, a photo, an upload or a link.
   I have nothing to give you. Everything you need is in the STORY LOCK.
⚠️ Do not ask me which style I want. Decide it from the story and write it.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 1 — THE FILM LOOKS MODERN AND THE PEOPLE LOOK YOUNG
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
This film must look like it was shot this year on a modern digital cinema
camera. Clean, sharp, rich. Not film grain, not vintage, not faded.

Every character must look their correct age. If a character is nineteen,
she must look nineteen — smooth skin, full cheeks, bright clear eyes.
Old-looking faces are the single biggest give-away that ruins these films.

BANNED WORDS — never write these about a person:
  weathered · aged · lined · wrinkled · gaunt · tired · haggard ·
  sun-damaged · worn · leathery · faded · dusty · ragged

BANNED WORDS — never write these about a place or a thing:
  faded · peeling · cracked · crumbling · decayed · dusty ·
  tarnished · worn · stained · dilapidated · overgrown

An old setting is made by its OBJECTS, not by decay. A village is old
because of clay pots, hand-woven cloth, oil lamps and mud walls — not
because everything looks dirty and broken.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 2 — NEVER WRITE A NAME, ALWAYS WRITE THE PERSON
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Do not write "Krishna", "Radha", "Shiva", "Ram" or any name inside a
prompt. The moment a name is written, the tool returns poster art — flat,
glowing, painted. Write the actual person instead: their age, build, skin,
hair, clothes, jewellery. The viewer will recognise them instantly from
the description alone. The tool needs a photograph brief, not a genre.

  ❌ "Lord Krishna under a kadamba tree, divine, devotional"
  ✅ "a young man of about twenty, lean and broad-shouldered, skin an
      even deep blue-grey with visible pores, shoulder-length curling
      black hair, a tall worked-gold crown holding one peacock feather,
      a saffron silk dhoti with a woven gold border creased where it is
      tied, a bamboo flute in both hands"

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 3 — DIVINE IS MADE OF MATERIALS, NOT LIGHT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
If a character is a god or a legendary figure, they look magnificent
because of what they wear and carry — silk, gold, gems, flowers, feathers —
and because of how they stand and move. Never because of glow.

BANNED: glowing · luminous · radiant · aura · halo · god rays ·
light shafts · divine light · ethereal · dreamlike · floating petals ·
bloom · lens flare · rim glow · golden mist

A divine figure is lit like a real person in a real place: one light
source, one direction, real shadow on one side of the face.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
RULE 4 — WRITE POSITIVES ONLY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Never write "no ..." anything. The tool reads the noun and gives you
the thing. "A room with no furniture" produces furniture.

  ❌ no glow          ✅ clear dry air; every surface lit only by direct
                         light and its own cast shadow
  ❌ no plastic skin  ✅ healthy skin with fine natural texture, matte
                         surface, uneven natural colour
  ❌ no text          ✅ every surface blank and unmarked
  ❌ no extra people  ✅ exactly one person in the frame

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
HOW MANY CHARACTERS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
A character who appears in two or more shots needs a DNA line.
Someone in the background of one shot does not.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
NOW GIVE ME YOUR OUTPUT IN THESE FIVE PARTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
┃  PART A — THE LOOK                                      ┃
┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛
  One paragraph describing how this film looks. The camera, the lens
  family, the light, the colour. Decide it from the story. Then one line:
  "This film looks like: ______."

  And the PALETTE: 4 or 5 colours this film may use. Nothing outside
  this palette appears anywhere in the film.

┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
┃  PART B — CHARACTER DNA  ⭐                             ┃
┃  (this is what stops the face changing)                 ┃
┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

  For each character write ONE single unbroken line. This exact line
  gets pasted, word for word, into every image prompt that character
  appears in. Never shortened. Never reworded. Written as one paragraph.

  Every DNA line must contain, in this exact order:
    1. AGE AND BUILD — start with the age. "a young woman of about
       nineteen, slight and quick-moving"
    2. SKIN — tone and texture, healthy, with natural texture
    3. FACE — shape, eyes, eyebrows, mouth. The features, not adjectives.
       "a narrow oval face, large dark almond eyes, strong straight
       brows, a small mouth with a full lower lip"
    4. HAIR — length, colour, texture, exactly how it is tied, what is
       in it, and where the loose strands fall
    5. CLOTHES — every garment, its colour, its fabric, how it sits on
       the body, and the exact state of its folds and drape
    6. JEWELLERY — every piece, one by one, by name, with its material
    7. CONSTANT PROP — anything they always carry or hold

  Write each in this shape:

    NAME = [full DNA line]

  Example of the correct length and detail:

    LALITA = a young woman of about nineteen, slight and quick-moving,
    warm brown skin with fine natural texture and a healthy sheen on
    the cheekbones, a narrow oval face, large dark almond eyes with
    long lashes, strong straight brows, a small mouth with a full
    lower lip, long black hair in one thick braid over her left
    shoulder with four loose strands loose at the temple, a deep red
    cotton choli and a saffron ghagra with a fine gold border, the
    odhni currently over her head and across both shoulders, small
    silver jhumkas, a small red bindi, thin silver bangles on both
    wrists, bare feet, always carrying a small brass puja thali

  ⚠️ If a character changes clothes or ages during the film, write a
  SEPARATE full DNA line for each version and say which shots use which.
  A costume change must be SHOWN happening — never a silent change.

  ⚠️ If a character is divine, the DNA line still describes a real person
  in real materials. Magnificence comes from the items, in detail.

┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
┃  PART C — CHARACTER PORTRAIT PROMPTS                    ┃
┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

  For each character, ONE portrait prompt. This is what I will actually
  upload to the video tool. It must be a clean, single, real photograph
  of one person — nothing else on the canvas.

  Each portrait prompt must contain, in this order:
    • "Photographed on a modern digital cinema camera, ARRI Alexa 35,
      85mm lens at T2.0, medium close-up, camera height 150cm"
    • the FULL DNA line, word for word
    • "exactly one person in the frame, nobody else, plain uncluttered
      background far behind them, soft even light from the front left
      so the right side of the face falls into gentle real shadow"
    • "healthy skin with fine natural texture, individual hairs visible,
      sharp focus on the eyes, rich colour, deep blacks, clean bright
      highlights"
    • "vertical 9:16, every surface blank and unmarked"

  Say clearly: "Save each as CHAR-<name>.png. This is the only image you
  ever upload for this character."

┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
┃  PART D — LOCATION DNA                                  ┃
┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

  For every place in the route map, one line of 40-60 words describing
  it as a real physical location: the ground, what is on the left, what
  is on the right, what is ahead, what is overhead, and the one thing
  that makes this place unmistakably itself.

  Format: PLACE NAME = [line]

┏━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┓
┃  PART E — WORKING REFERENCE SHEETS (not for uploading)  ┃
┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━┛

  ⚠️ These sheets are for MY eyes only. I use them to check continuity.
  They never go into the video tool — a sheet in the tool produces flat
  drawn looking output.

  For each character, one sheet prompt:
    "Character reference sheet, four views of the same person in one
```

```
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
THE FOUR LAWS THAT MAKE THIS LOOK LIKE A REAL FILM
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

━━━ LAW 1 — ACTING IS THE MOST IMPORTANT THING IN THIS FILM ━━━
A small story with great faces works. A great story with blank faces
does not.

A real actor does not hold one expression. A real face MOVES through an
emotion. Every card must write that movement, second by second.

BANNED — never write these as the whole description of a face:
  smiles · looks happy · is sad · reacts · surprised · divine expression ·
  shows emotion · emotionally moved · bittersweet · serene

Instead, write the muscles and the sequence. Follow this engine:

  THOUGHT      What is she thinking right now — in her own words, not
               an emotion word. "He is going to say no and I already
               know it." This is what puts something behind the eyes.
  TURN         The face travels from one state to another. Name both
               ends and the SECOND it happens.
               "at 0-1s her face is closed and polite; at 2s it breaks
               into a real grin and she looks away immediately"
  LEAK         One thing she cannot control. One only. A swallow, a
               blink that comes late, a breath that catches, the corner
               of the mouth going up before she stops it.
  BUSY BODY    What her hands are doing while her face is working.
               Nobody's hands hang still.

⭐ THE SUPPRESSION RULE: an emotion that is FELT and HELD BACK is real.
   An emotion that is announced is fake. She tries not to smile and fails
   — that is a performance. She smiles — that is a photograph.
   But do not over-suppress: joy still has to arrive on the face and be
   allowed to live there for a moment before it is controlled.

⭐ THE LATE REACTION RULE: a real reaction arrives a beat after the
   thing that caused it. Write the gap: "she hears it — 0-1s nothing —
   2s her eyes come up."

⭐ EYES OR HEAD FIRST: in every reaction, decide which moves first and
   write it. This one detail is the difference between acting and a
   frozen face.

━━━ LAW 2 — NOBODY PERFORMS FOR THE CAMERA ━━━
Every shot is a person reacting to the OTHER PERSON, not to the lens.
Nobody looks at the camera. Nobody poses. Nobody holds a pretty face.

If a shot has two people, write what each one NOTICES about the other,
and what they do about it. The relationship is the subject. The scenery
is only where it happens.

━━━ LAW 3 — THE HANDOVER: THIS IS HOW CUTS DISAPPEAR ━━━
Whatever is moving at the end of one shot is the same thing moving at
the start of the next shot, seen from a new angle. Six ways to do it —
use at least four different ones across the film:

  1. MOVEMENT MATCH — she is walking out of frame right; the next shot
     she is still walking, still moving right, new angle
  2. ACTION MATCH — a hand reaches toward a curtain; the next shot the
     same hand touches the curtain, other side
  3. EYELINE MATCH — she looks up and left; the next shot shows what is
     up and left, from her height
  4. SHAPE MATCH — the curve of her veil; the next shot opens on the
     same curve of leaves
  5. COLOUR MATCH — the green of her dress; the next shot is the green
     of the forest filling the frame
  6. CONSEQUENCE MATCH — she drops a bracelet; the next shot begins on
     the bracelet on the ground, and she is already walking away past it
     (this one is the strongest — the object carries the story forward)

Every card must state its handover in one line:
  "CONNECTS FROM: [what was moving at the end of the previous shot]"
  "HANDS TO: [what is still moving at the end of this shot]"

A shot that begins and ends at rest, in the middle of the film, is a
dead shot. Only the final shot may come to rest.

━━━ LAW 4 — DISTANCE MEANS SOMETHING ━━━
Camera size is chosen from emotion, never for variety:
  reaction → close-up, camera still
  the biggest emotional moment → extreme close-up
  power, divinity → low angle looking up
  shame, defeat → high angle looking down
  two people's relationship → two-shot or over-the-shoulder
  loneliness → wide, the figure small in the frame
  action, travel → full body or wide, often from behind

⚠️ Any emotion on the face must be FRONT or THREE-QUARTER. A profile
hides half the performance, and a wide shot hides all of it.

⚠️ Always state how high the camera is off the ground. If you do not,
it renders flat at chest height every time and the shot loses its meaning.

Never give two neighbouring shots the same size and the same move.

moves drift. Always add a speed and a distance.

  Everyday:  Static · Dolly In · Dolly Out · Truck Left · Truck Right ·

             Follow · Track · Rack Focus
  Shaped:    Crane Up · Crane Down · Jib · Arc · Orbit · Lazy Susan ·

  Framing:   Two-Shot · Over-The-Shoulder · POV · Dutch Angle

  Write it like: "Dolly In, very slow, one metre, no cuts"

🔒 ACROSS THE WHOLE FILM: exactly ONE bold move. A bold move is a punch.
Throw one and it lands. Throw six and it is only noise. Everything else
is Static, Dolly, Truck, Pan or Follow.

🔒 SPEED RAMP: exactly one in the whole film, at the peak moment.

  • what moves on her
  • what is left behind in the air
  • what her face does about it

🔒 Fire is an event. A glow is a filter. Events yes. Glow never.
```
